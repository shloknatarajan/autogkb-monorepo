# LitSuggest Triage Evaluation Report

**Model:** gpt-5.2  
**Date:** 2026-06-01  
**Coverage:** 500/500 ground truth records (100%)

---

## Background

AutoGKB uses LitSuggest — NCBI's ML-based paper recommendation system — to surface candidate articles for PharmGKB curation. Each week, LitSuggest produces a digest of papers scored 0–100 for relevance to clinical pharmacogenomics. The AutoGKB pipeline runs those papers through an LLM triage step that assigns each article one of three labels:

- **`relevant`** — high-confidence pharmacogenomics content, prioritize for curation
- **`borderline`** — possible relevance, surface for human review
- **`not_relevant`** — skip

This report evaluates how accurately the LLM triage step reproduces PharmGKB curators' own judgments on the same papers.

---

## Methodology

### Ground Truth

The ground truth dataset (`data/litsuggest/LitSuggest_in_ClinPGx.tsv`) contains 500 articles previously reviewed by PharmGKB curators, each tagged with one of five curation states. These were mapped to the pipeline's three-class taxonomy:

| PharmGKB State | Pipeline Label |
|---|---|
| Curated | `relevant` |
| Relevant | `borderline` |
| Relevant Association | `borderline` |
| Not Curated | `not_relevant` |
| Not Relevant | `not_relevant` |

Class distribution: 189 relevant (37.8%), 150 borderline (30.0%), 161 not_relevant (32.2%).

### Scoring

Triage scores were generated by running the full AutoGKB pipeline (gpt-5.2, `score_for_va` prompt) on all 29 General PGx weekly digest jobs from November 2025 through May 2026 — a total of 682 scored PMIDs. The 500 ground truth PMIDs that appeared in those digests were used for evaluation. Scores were sourced from the `triage_sessions` PostgreSQL table rather than making new LLM calls; when a PMID appeared in multiple sessions, the most recent score was used.

### Metrics

Three complementary views were computed:

1. **3-class accuracy** — exact match rate across all three labels
2. **Per-label precision/recall/F1** — standard binary classification metrics computed per class (one-vs-rest)
3. **Binary (curator workflow) view** — collapses `relevant` + `borderline` → "worth reviewing" vs. `not_relevant` → "skip", reflecting the actual curator decision: does this paper deserve a look?

---

## Interpretation

### What the model does well

**It rarely surfaces irrelevant papers.** Precision of 0.857 in the binary view means that when the model flags something as worth reviewing, it's right 86% of the time. Out of 161 truly irrelevant papers, only 42 were incorrectly surfaced — a false positive rate of 26%. For a curation pipeline where human time is the scarce resource, this is important: curators aren't drowning in false leads.

**It correctly skips most junk.** Of 161 papers that PharmGKB deemed not worth curating, the model correctly buried 119 (74%). The 17 papers it falsely promoted to `relevant` are the most concerning misclassifications, but they still represent only 11% of the not_relevant class.

### Where it struggles

**Recall is the main weakness.** The model misses 26% of papers worth reviewing (88 out of 339). These are papers a curator would want to see that the model buries as `not_relevant`. For a literature triage tool, false negatives are the higher-stakes error: a paper missed now may never resurface.

**`borderline` is the hardest class.** F1 of 0.429 — substantially below the other two classes. The confusion matrix shows that borderline articles bleed in both directions: 33 are over-promoted to `relevant`, 55 are under-promoted to `not_relevant`. This reflects a real ambiguity: the `borderline` label itself (PharmGKB's "Relevant" state) describes papers that human curators flagged but didn't fully process, which is inherently a noisy signal.

**The model is biased toward `not_relevant`.** Recall for `not_relevant` (0.739) exceeds recall for `relevant` (0.550) and `borderline` (0.413), suggesting the model has a conservative bias — when uncertain, it tends to suppress rather than surface. This is the inverse of what a recall-optimized literature triage tool would want.

---

## Potential Improvements

### 1. Calibrate toward recall
The conservative bias (over-predicting `not_relevant`) is the most actionable finding. Options:

- **Lower the `not_relevant` score threshold** — currently anything scoring 0–39 is `not_relevant`. Raising the cutoff to, say, 0–29 would force borderline-scoring papers into `borderline` rather than `not_relevant`.
- **Adjust the triage prompt** to instruct the model to err on the side of surfacing when uncertain. A phrase like "when in doubt, classify as borderline rather than not_relevant" would directly target the false-negative rate.

### 2. Improve `borderline` discrimination
The `borderline` class has the worst F1 (0.429). The underlying issue is that "Relevant" in PharmGKB is weakly defined — curators tag a paper as potentially interesting without committing to it. The LLM prompt could be refined with more explicit criteria for what makes something borderline vs. relevant (e.g., "the paper studies a specific drug-gene interaction with human outcome data" = relevant; "the paper mentions pharmacogenomics but focuses on methodology" = borderline).

### 3. Multi-pass scoring
Currently each paper is scored once with a single prompt. A two-step approach — first classify as `relevant/not_relevant`, then apply a second prompt only to positives to distinguish `relevant` from `borderline` — could improve the borderline boundary at the cost of extra LLM calls.

### 4. Expand ground truth coverage
The 500-record ground truth dataset covers only articles that LitSuggest surfaced AND that PharmGKB reviewed. Articles that LitSuggest scored poorly but that PharmGKB would have curated (true recall gap) are invisible in this evaluation. A more complete benchmark would include PharmGKB-curated articles that LitSuggest *didn't* surface.

### 5. Temporal validation
All 29 sessions in this evaluation span November 2025 – May 2026. The ground truth was collected from an earlier period (the ClinPGx dataset). Testing on held-out future weeks would give a cleaner estimate of out-of-distribution performance.

### 6. Evaluate across models
This run used gpt-5.2. The same backfill infrastructure (`litsuggest-backfill --projects`) can re-run with other models (e.g., `claude-opus-4-7`, `gpt-4o`) to build a model comparison table. Given the conservative bias observed, a model with stronger instruction-following may better respect "borderline" as a distinct class.
